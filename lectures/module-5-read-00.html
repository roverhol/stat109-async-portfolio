In Module 5 you will fit a line to two numerical variables and use it to predict. That only makes sense when the relationship is roughly linear, and the tools you use to decide that are exactly the ones from Module 3:

  • the scatterplot (to see the form), and
  • the Pearson correlation \(r\) (to summarize the strength and direction of a linear form).

Reading a scatterplot: form, direction, strength

For two numerical variables, put the explanatory variable on the \(x\)-axis (what we have to predict from) and the response on the \(y\)-axis (what we want to predict), then describe three things:

  • Form, linear, curved, or no clear pattern.
  • Direction, positive (up to the right), negative (down to the right), or none.
  • Strength, how tightly the points cluster around the trend (strong, moderate, weak).

Always note unusual points (outliers) too, since they can distort everything that follows.

Pearson correlation \(r\)

Pearson correlation \(r\) is a single, unitless number between \(-1\) and \(1\) that summarizes the direction and strength of a linear relationship:

  • The sign gives direction: \(r > 0\) positive, \(r < 0\) negative.
  • The magnitude \(|r|\) gives strength: near \(1\) is a tight linear trend, near \(0\) is little or no linear trend.

For sample data \((x_i, y_i)\), \(i = 1, \ldots, n\):

\[ r = \frac{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})(y_i - \bar{y})} {\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n (x_i - \bar{x})^2}\;\sqrt{\sum_{i=1}^n (y_i - \bar{y})^2}} \]

The numerator is large and positive when \(x\) and \(y\) tend to be above (or below) their means together; the denominator rescales by the spread in each variable so the result always lands between \(-1\) and \(1\).

Warning\(r\) only describes a linear trend

A correlation near \(0\) does not mean “no relationship”, it means “no linear relationship.” A strong curved pattern (a U-shape, say) can have \(r \approx 0\). And a single outlier can pull \(r\) toward or away from \(\pm 1\). Always look at the scatterplot before trusting \(r\).

In R: cor() and a scatterplot

Code 
library(tidyverse)

set.seed(509)
review_dat <- tibble(
  study_hours = runif(60, 1, 15),
  exam_score  = 45 + 3.4 * study_hours + rnorm(60, 0, 8)
)

ggplot(review_dat, aes(x = study_hours, y = exam_score)) +
  geom_point(alpha = 0.8, color = "steelblue4") +
  labs(title = "Exam score vs study hours",
       x = "Study hours", y = "Exam score") +
  theme_minimal()
Code 
cor(review_dat$study_hours, review_dat$exam_score)
[1] 0.817906

Here the form is roughly linear, the direction is positive, and \(r\) is moderately strong, exactly the situation where fitting a line (Read 1) is reasonable.

## In R: `cor()` and a scatterplot

```{r}

library(tidyverse)

set.seed(509)
review_dat <- tibble(
  study_hours = runif(60, 1, 15),
```
