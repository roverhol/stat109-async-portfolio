**Big question:** What is the truth about a **whole population**, and how close can we get from one **sample**?

## The story in one pass

In Module 1 you tested a **claim** with a p-value. In Module 2 you **estimate** an unknown **parameter** (population proportion $p$ or mean $\mu$) using a **statistic** from your sample ($\hat{p}$ or $\bar{x}$).

A **95% confidence interval** is a range of plausible values for the parameter. The interval width reflects **precision** — larger $n$ usually means a narrower interval. **How** you sample matters too: a **simple random sample** from a good **sampling frame** supports generalizing to the population; convenience samples often do not.

Project 2 requires you to define a **finite, listable population** ($N$), draw an **SRS** of size $n$, compute the right **95% CI** (mean or proportion; **FPC** when sampling without replacement from finite $N$), and interpret it with the sentence pattern from Read 1.
