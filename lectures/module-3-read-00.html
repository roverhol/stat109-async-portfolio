**Big question:** What does a dataset *look like*, and what patterns hide inside it?

:::

## The story in one pass

In Modules 1–2 you had **one** variable at a time: a proportion to test, or a mean to estimate. In Module 3 you explore a **real dataset** with multiple variables by making **graphs** and computing **numeric summaries** for each variable — and then for pairs of variables together.

**Exploratory data analysis (EDA)** is not about hypothesis testing. It is about building an honest picture of what the data contain before drawing conclusions. The four key tools for Project 3:

| Variable types | Graph | Numeric summary |
|----------------|-------|-----------------|
| One numerical | Histogram or dot plot | Mean, median, SD, IQR |
| One categorical | Bar chart | Count, proportion |
| Numerical by group | Boxplot per group | `group_by()` + `summarize()` |
| Two numerical | Scatterplot | Form, direction, strength |
| Two categorical | Stacked bar chart | Two-way table (row/column proportions) |

Project 3 is **descriptive only** — inferential tests (t-test, etc.) are optional enrichment, not required.

## What makes a good Project 3 dataset

A good dataset for Project 3 has:

- **≥ 4 variables** of different types (at least one numerical, at least one categorical)
- **≥ 10 rows** of observational units (more is fine)
- A **documented source** (TidyTuesday, government open data, UCI ML repository, etc.)
- Variables with a real-world story worth telling
