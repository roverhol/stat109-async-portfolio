Module 4, Read 0: Why fair comparisons are hard

Project: Project 4, Designing Fair Comparisons · Next: Read 1

The central question of Module 4

Almost every interesting claim in science and public policy involves comparing two groups:

  • Does this drug work better than a placebo?
  • Does the tutoring program raise test scores?
  • Are workers in remote roles more productive than in-office workers?

These questions look straightforward. They are not. How you compare two groups determines whether your answer means anything.

What you will do in Project 4

Project 4 assigns you a research question about a two-group comparison. You will:

  1. Classify the design, Is it an RCT or an observational study? Who decides group membership?
  2. Identify confounders, Draw a simple DAG showing what else might explain the difference.
  3. Simulate a dataset, Use set.seed() to create a reproducible, fictional dataset that mirrors the design.
  4. Run the right test, Pick from the methods table by variable types (numerical → Welch t; binary → two-proportion z).
  5. Write for a classmate, Summarize your findings in one page for someone who asked the question but is not a statistician.

Your project stays limited to a single two-group comparison (one binary explanatory contrast). For a complete worked example, see the Project 4 sample R notebook (simulate → describe → test → check the confounder).

Three things that can go wrong when comparing groups

Tip1. Confounding

A third variable affects both who ends up in each group and the outcome. Observational comparisons are vulnerable to confounding, even with large samples and small p-values.

Tip2. Using the wrong test

Comparing two group means? Use the Welch two-sample t-test. Comparing two group proportions? Use a two-proportion z-test or chi-square. The methods map will guide you.

Tip3. Overclaiming causation

A p-value of 0.001 does not mean the treatment caused the difference in an observational study. Causal language requires a randomized experiment (or very careful design).
