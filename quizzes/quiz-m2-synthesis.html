Section A — Interpret confidence intervals

Use: “I am 95% confident that [parameter] in the entire population is between [L] and [U]. In this context, confidence means that if data from many samples were collected the same way, then 95% of the intervals computed from these samples would contain the true parameter.”

A1. First-generation proportion

95% CI for proportion of undergraduates who are first-generation: [0.22, 0.30]. Interpret in context.

A2. Website minutes mean

95% CI for mean minutes/day on course websites: [41, 55] minutes. Interpret.

A3. Schools older than 50 years

95% CI with FPC for proportion of schools older than 50 years: [0.14, 0.27]. Interpret.

A4. Spot incorrect interpretation

Which is the best interpretation of a 95% CI?

  1. 95% probability the parameter is in this interval.
  2. Repeated sampling: ~95% of such intervals contain the parameter.
  3. 95% of individuals fall between the endpoints.

C2. prop.test — 95% CI endpoints

Using the same seatbelt study (x = 332, n = 400), R prints:

95 percent confidence interval:
 0.7879343 0.8720657

Which is the best approximate 95% CI for the population proportion?

  1. [0.79, 0.87]
  2. [0.33, 0.40]
  3. [0.79, 0.83]
  4. [332, 400]

C3. prop.test — p-value conclusion

A one-sided test uses prop.test(x = 62, n = 100, p = 0.5, alternative = "greater") and reports p-value < 2.2e-16. At \(\alpha = 0.05\), what is the best conclusion about \(H_0: p = 0.5\) vs \(H_a: p > 0.5\)?

  1. Reject \(H_0\) — strong evidence the population proportion exceeds 0.5
  2. Fail to reject \(H_0\) — not enough evidence
  3. Accept \(H_0\) as proven true
  4. The p-value is the sample proportion

C4. Wald large-count check

For prop.test / Wald on \(n = 400\), \(x = 332\), do large-count conditions hold?

  1. Yes — \(n\hat{p}\) and \(n(1-\hat{p})\) are both \(\geq 10\)
  2. No — need \(n > 400\)
  3. No — only successes matter
  4. Yes — only when FPC is applied

C5. t.test — sample mean

Library hours for \(n = 6\) campuses:

t.test(c(8, 10, 12, 12, 14, 16))
...
mean of x           12

What is the single best estimate of the population mean from these data?

  1. \(\mu = 12\) (known parameter)
  2. \(\bar{x} = 12\) (sample statistic)
  3. Margin of error \(= 12\)
  4. p-value \(= 12\)

C6. t.test — interpret CI

For the same library-hour sample, R reports:

95 percent confidence interval:
  9.483032 14.516968

Best one-sentence interpretation?

  1. I am 95% confident the true population mean weekly open hours is between 9.48 and 14.52 hours.
  2. There is a 95% probability \(\mu\) is in this interval.
  3. 95% of libraries have hours in this range.
  4. We proved \(\mu = 12\) exactly.

C7. t.test — when and why

SRS of \(n = 6\) library hours from finite \(N\); \(\sigma\) unknown. Why is t.test() appropriate?

  1. \(s\) is estimated from the sample; \(t^*\) adjusts for small \(n\) when \(\sigma\) is unknown
  2. Proportions always need binom.test()
  3. FPC replaces the need for any conditions
  4. Mean CIs never require a sampling design check

C8. binom.test vs prop.test

Hospital mortality: \(n = 16\) patients, \(x = 3\) deaths. Best 95% CI function?

  1. binom.test() — Wald large-count conditions fail
  2. prop.test() only — always Wald
  3. t.test()
  4. No CI is possible
