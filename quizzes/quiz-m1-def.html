Section A — Probability language (Read 1 §1)

A1. Counts from data

A student flips a fair coin 20 times and observes 9 heads. What are \(x\) and \(n\)?

A2. Observed proportion

A fair coin is tossed 20 times and 9 heads are observed, so \(x = 9\) and \(n = 20\). Compute \(\hat{p}\). Show your calculation.

A3. Percentage

In a study, the observed sample proportion of successes is \(\hat{p} = 0.45\). Convert this to a percentage.

A4. Data vs theory

A fair coin is tossed 20 times and 9 heads are observed (\(\hat{p} = 9/20 = 0.45\)). For a fair coin, the long-run probability of heads is \(0.5\). Using correct notation, state the observed proportion of heads and the theoretical probability of heads.

A5. \(\hat{p}\) vs \(P(\text{event})\)

In one or two sentences, explain the difference between \(\hat{p}\) and \(P(\text{event})\). Refer to data and long-run behavior.

A6. Definition of probability

State the definition of probability involving a repeatable random process, a proportion, and the idea that the process continues forever.

Click to reveal answers — Section A — Probability language (Read 1 §1)

  1. \(x = 9\) (count of heads); \(n = 20\) (total tosses).

  2. \(\hat{p} = x/n = 9/20 = 0.45\).

  3. \(45\%\).

  4. Observed: \(\hat{p} = 0.45\). Theoretical: \(P(\text{head}) = 0.5\).

  5. \(\hat{p}\) summarizes observed data from a fixed sample. \(P(\text{event})\) describes long-run behavior of the random process.

  6. \(P(\text{Event})\) is the proportion of times the event occurs when the trial is repeated infinitely many times.

Section B — Sample space, outcomes, and events (Read 1 §2)

B1. Vocabulary

Give a clear definition of each term:

  1. Sample space
  2. Outcome
  3. Event

B2. Die trial vocabulary

One roll of a fair six-sided die:

  1. Write the sample space \(S\).
  2. Give one outcome.
  3. Give one event (and list the outcomes in it).

B3. Set language

Suppose a trial has sample space \(S\) and events \(A\) and \(B\).

  1. In words, what does “\(A\) and \(B\)” mean?
  2. In words, what does “\(A\) or \(B\)” mean?
  3. In words, what does “not \(A\)” mean?

B4. Translate notation to English

Write a one-sentence interpretation for each:

  1. \(P(A) = 0.7\)
  2. \(P(A \text{ and } B) = 0.2\)
  3. \(P(\text{not } A) = 0.1\)
Click to reveal answers — Section B — Sample space, outcomes, and events (Read 1 §2)
    1. Sample space \(S\): all possible outcomes of a trial. b) Outcome: one element of \(S\). c) Event: a set of one or more outcomes.

  2. \(S = \{1,2,3,4,5,6\}\); e.g. outcome 4; e.g. \(A = \{2,4,6\}\) (even).

  3. And: both occur. Or: at least one occurs. Not \(A\): \(A\) does not occur.

    1. There is a 70% chance event \(A\) occurs. 2. There is a 20% chance both \(A\) and \(B\) occur. 3. There is a 10% chance \(A\) does not occur.

Section C — Equally likely outcomes (Read 1 §2)

C1. Equally likely rule

State the probability rule for event \(A\) when all outcomes in \(S\) are equally likely. Define \(|A|\) and \(|S|\) in words.

C2. When the rule applies

State the condition that must be true for \(P(A) = |A|/|S|\) to be valid.

Click to reveal answers — Section C — Equally likely outcomes (Read 1 §2)

  1. \(P(A) = |A|/|S|\) where \(|A|\) = number of outcomes in \(A\), \(|S|\) = number in the sample space.

  2. All outcomes in the sample space must be equally likely.

Section D — Addition rule (Read 1 §3)

D1. Addition rule

State the addition rule for events \(A\) and \(B\).

D2. Backpacks and hats

65 students: 40 have backpacks, 10 have hats, 5 have both. Let \(A\) = has backpack, \(B\) = has hat.

  1. Find \(P(A)\) and \(P(B)\).
  2. Find \(P(A \text{ and } B)\).
  3. Use the addition rule to find \(P(A \text{ or } B)\).

D3. Complement

If \(P(\text{rains at least once in July}) = 0.91\), find \(P(\text{no rain in July})\).

D4. Disjoint events

Suppose \(A\) and \(B\) are non-overlapping (mutually exclusive).

  1. What does “non-overlapping” mean in words?
  2. What is \(P(A \text{ and } B)\)?
  3. Write the simplified formula for \(P(A \text{ or } B)\).
Click to reveal answers — Section D — Addition rule (Read 1 §3)

  1. \(P(A \text{ or } B) = P(A) + P(B) - P(A \text{ and } B)\).

  2. \(P(A)=40/65\), \(P(B)=10/65\), \(P(A\text{ and }B)=5/65\), \(P(A\text{ or }B)=45/65\).

  3. \(1 - 0.91 = 0.09\).

  4. They cannot both occur; \(P(A\text{ and }B)=0\); \(P(A\text{ or }B)=P(A)+P(B)\).

Section E — Conditional probability and independence (Read 1 §4)

E1. Conditional probability

  1. In words, what does \(P(A \mid B)\) mean?
  2. State the formula for \(P(A \mid B)\) when \(P(B) > 0\).

E2. Multiplication rule

  1. State the multiplication rule for \(P(A \text{ and } B)\).
  2. State the special case when \(A\) and \(B\) are independent.

E3. Independence

  1. In words, when are \(A\) and \(B\) independent?
  2. Give two equivalent ways to check independence.

E4. Translate conditional notation

Write a one-sentence interpretation for each:

  1. \(P(A \mid B) = 0.3\)
  2. \(P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A)\,P(B)\)

E5. Match notation with English

Define \(A\) = has the disease, \(B\) = tests positive. Match each expression to the correct interpretation:

Expression Interpretation
\(P(A \text{ and } B)\) (a) Baseline chance of disease before any test
\(P(A)\) (b) Among positive tests, chance of disease
\(P(A \mid B)\) (c) Proportion with disease and positive test
\(P(B \mid A)\) (d) Among diseased, chance test is positive
\(P(A \text{ or } B)\) (e) Has disease, or tests positive, or both

E6. Die conditionals

Fair die rolled once:

  1. Find \(P(\text{roll is 2} \mid \text{roll is even})\).
  2. Find \(P(\text{roll is even} \mid \text{roll is 2})\).
Click to reveal answers — Section E — Conditional probability and independence (Read 1 §4)

  1. Probability of \(A\) given \(B\) occurred: \(P(A \mid B) = P(A \text{ and } B)/P(B)\).

  2. \(P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A \mid B)P(B)\); if independent, \(P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A)P(B)\).

  3. Knowing one does not change the other’s probability; e.g. \(P(A \mid B) = P(A)\) or \(P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A)P(B)\).

    1. Given \(B\) occurred, there is a 30% chance \(A\) occurs. 2. \(A\) and \(B\) are independent (joint equals product of marginals).

  5. \(P(A\text{ and }B)\to(c)\); \(P(A)\to(a)\); \(P(A\mid B)\to(b)\); \(P(B\mid A)\to(d)\); \(P(A\text{ or }B)\to(e)\).

    1. \(1/3\). 2. \(1\) (if the roll is 2, it is certainly even).

## Section E — Conditional probability and independence (Read 1 §4)

### E1. Conditional probability

1. In words, what does $P(A \mid B)$ mean?
2. State the formula for $P(A \mid B)$ when $P(B) > 0$.

### E2. Multiplication rule

1. State the **multiplication rule** for $P(A \text{ and } B)$.
2. State the **special case** when $A$ and $B$ are independent.

### E3. Independence

1. In words, when are $A$ and $B$ **independent**?
2. Give **two** equivalent ways to check independence.

### E4. Translate conditional notation

Write a one-sentence interpretation for each:

1. $P(A \mid B) = 0.3$
2. $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A)\,P(B)$

### E5. Match notation with English

Define $A$ = has the disease, $B$ = tests positive. **Match** each expression to the correct interpretation:

| Expression | Interpretation |
|------------|----------------|
| $P(A \text{ and } B)$ | (a) Baseline chance of disease before any test |
| $P(A)$ | (b) Among positive tests, chance of disease |
| $P(A \mid B)$ | (c) Proportion with disease **and** positive test |
| $P(B \mid A)$ | (d) Among diseased, chance test is positive |
| $P(A \text{ or } B)$ | (e) Has disease, or tests positive, or both |

### E6. Die conditionals

Fair die rolled once:

1. Find $P(\text{roll is 2} \mid \text{roll is even})$.
2. Find $P(\text{roll is even} \mid \text{roll is 2})$.

::: {.content-visible when-format="html"}
<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers — Section E — Conditional probability and independence (Read 1 §4)</summary>

1. Probability of $A$ given $B$ occurred: $P(A \mid B) = P(A \text{ and } B)/P(B)$.

2. $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A \mid B)P(B)$; if independent, $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A)P(B)$.

3. Knowing one does not change the other's probability; e.g. $P(A \mid B) = P(A)$ or $P(A \text{ and } B) = P(A)P(B)$.

4. 1. Given $B$ occurred, there is a 30% chance $A$ occurs. 2. $A$ and $B$ are independent (joint equals product of marginals).

5. $P(A\text{ and }B)\to(c)$; $P(A)\to(a)$; $P(A\mid B)\to(b)$; $P(B\mid A)\to(d)$; $P(A\text{ or }B)\to(e)$.

6. 1. $1/3$. 2. $1$ (if the roll is 2, it is certainly even).

</details>
:::
