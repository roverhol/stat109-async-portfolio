Section B — Methods-map matching (Read 2 §1)

B1. Two numerical groups

Two independent groups; outcome is blood pressure (numerical). Which test and R function?

B2. Two binary groups

Two groups; outcome is recovered (yes/no). Sample sizes \(n_1=80\), \(n_2=75\). Which test and R function?

B3. Matched pairs vs independent

Same students tested before and after an intervention. Which design — matched pairs or independent groups? Which test?

B4. Expected counts condition

When using chisq.test(), what condition must you check?

B5. Welch vs equal-variance t

Why does R’s t.test() use the Welch approximation by default (not equal-variance t)?

Click to reveal answers — Section B — Methods-map matching (Read 2 §1)

  1. Welch two-sample t-test. R: t.test(bp ~ group, data = df). Conditions: independence, reasonably normal or large \(n\) per group.

  2. Two-proportion z-test or chi-square test of independence. R: prop.test(c(x1, x2), c(80, 75)) or chisq.test(table(df$group, df$recovered)).

  3. Matched pairs (same units, two measurements). Use a paired t-test on differences: t.test(df$post - df$pre) or t.test(post, pre, paired = TRUE).

  4. All expected cell counts ≥ 5 (or ≥ 1 by relaxed rule). If violated, use Fisher’s exact test or combine categories.

  5. Welch does not assume equal population variances across groups — more robust and appropriate unless you have strong evidence the variances are equal.

Section C — Read two-group R output (Read 2 §1)

C1. Welch t-test decision

Welch two-sample t-test: \(p = 0.03\), 95% CI \([1.2, 8.6]\) (Group B \(-\) Group A). State the decision (\(\alpha=0.05\)) and interpret the CI.

C2. Fail to reject

Two-proportion z-test: \(p = 0.18\), 95% CI \([-0.02, 0.10]\) (proportion difference). State the decision (\(\alpha=0.05\)).

C3. Identify null and alternative

Two groups; \(H_0\): mean blood pressure is the same in both groups. Write the null and alternative hypotheses using \(\mu_1\) and \(\mu_2\).

Click to reveal answers — Section C — Read two-group R output (Read 2 §1)

  1. Reject \(H_0\) (p < 0.05). We are 95% confident the population mean for Group B exceeds Group A by between 1.2 and 8.6 units.

  2. Fail to reject \(H_0\) (p > 0.05). Insufficient evidence of a difference in population proportions.

  3. \(H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2\) (or \(\mu_1 - \mu_2 = 0\)) vs \(H_a: \mu_1 \neq \mu_2\) (two-sided) or directed alternative if specified.

### B5. Welch vs equal-variance t

<summary>Click to reveal answers — Section B — Methods-map matching (Read 2 §1)</summary>

4. All **expected cell counts ≥ 5** (or ≥ 1 by relaxed rule). If violated, use Fisher's exact test or combine categories.

5. **Welch** does not assume equal population variances across groups — more robust and appropriate unless you have strong evidence the variances are equal.

</details>
