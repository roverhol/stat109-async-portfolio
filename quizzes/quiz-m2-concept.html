Section A — Convenience vs SRS & bias (Read 1 §4–5)

A1. Dining hall intercept

First 60 students outside one dining hall 12–1 p.m. surveyed about sleep.

  1. Convenience or SRS? 2. Bias likely? 3. Generalizability?

A2. Registrar SRS

Random-number draw of 120 unique IDs from full undergraduate registrar list.

  1. Convenience or SRS? 2. Bias likely? 3. Generalizability?

A3. Club meeting

40 students at pre-med club meeting surveyed about med-school plans.

  1. Convenience or SRS? 2. Bias likely? 3. Generalizability?

A4. Incomplete frame

SRS from declared-majors list only; undeclared students excluded.

  1. Design label? 2. Bias? 3. Generalizability?
Click to reveal answers — Section A — Convenience vs SRS & bias (Read 1 §4–5)

  1. Convenience. Yes — time/location sample differs from all students. Limited generalizability.

  2. SRS. Low bias if list matches population and response is good. Reasonable to generalize to list population.

  3. Convenience. Yes — club members differ from all undergraduates. Poor campus-wide generalizability.

  4. SRS from incomplete frame. Yes — undercoverage of undeclared. Generalize only to students on the frame.

Section B — SRS steps & interval type

B1. SRS workflow

List three ordered steps for simple random sampling, then apply them to estimate mean study hours for \(N=210\) STAT 109 students with \(n=30\).

B2. Large-state proportion

\(n=220\), \(x=82\) with part-time job; SRS from very large statewide population.

Mean or proportion? FPC? Conditions?

B3. Finite classroom mean

\(N=300\) classrooms, SRS \(n=30\) without replacement; mean chairs.

Mean or proportion? FPC? Conditions?

B4. Clinic proportion with FPC

\(N=80\) patients, \(n=50\) without replacement; Yes/No recovery proportion.

Mean or proportion? FPC? Conditions?

B5. Small count proportion

Large campus SRS; biology major indicator; \(\hat{p}=2/18\).

Mean or proportion? FPC? Conditions?

Click to reveal answers — Section B — SRS steps & interval type
    1. Number frame 1–210. 2) Draw 30 unique random IDs. 3) Collect data from those students.

  2. Proportion. No FPC. Likely OK: SRS + large counts (\(n\hat{p}\) and \(n(1-\hat{p})\) both \(\geq 10\)).

  3. Mean. Use FPC (\(n/N=0.10\)). Random sample; check normality or moderate \(n\).

  4. Proportion. Use FPC (large sampling fraction). Check large-count condition with observed \(x\).

  5. Proportion. No FPC (large campus). Large-count fails (\(x=2<10\)) — Wald normal CI questionable; consider exact/binomial methods.

Section C — z-scores & statistics toolbox (Read 2 §1–2)

C1. Annarose z-score

Women: \(\mu=64\), \(\sigma=2.5\) in. Annarose is 67 in. Compute her z-score.

C2. Compare z-scores

Newton: 72 in; men \(\mu=70\), \(\sigma=3\). Who is more unusually tall relative to their group — Annarose (\(z=1.2\)) or Newton?

C3. Standard normal probability

For \(Z\sim N(0,1)\), what does \(P(Z\leq 1.24)\) represent? Also find \(P(Z>1.24)\) (table or calculator).

C4. Coin test hypotheses

16 heads in 20 flips; test if coin is fair (two-sided). State \(H_0\), \(H_a\), and define \(p\) in words.

C5. Role of \(p_0\)

In \(z=(\hat{p}-p_0)/\sqrt{p_0(1-p_0)/n}\), what is \(p_0\)?

C6. prop.test() decision

When using prop.test() for a hypothesis test, which output value do you compare to \(\alpha\)?

C7. Descriptive vs inferential

One binary variable from a sample: give one descriptive summary and one inferential question answered by a CI.

C8. Variable types

Classify each as numerical or categorical (and binary if categorical):

  1. Stem diameter (cm)
  2. Soil type: sand/loam/clay
  3. Parasite present: yes/no
  4. Egg count per nest
Click to reveal answers — Section C — z-scores & statistics toolbox (Read 2 §1–2)

  1. \(z=(67-64)/2.5=1.2\) — 1.2 SD above the women’s mean.

  2. Newton: \(z=(72-70)/3\approx 0.67\). Annarose (larger z-score).

  3. Proportion at or below 1.24 (about 0.893). \(P(Z>1.24)\approx 0.107\).

  4. \(H_0:p=0.5\), \(H_a:p\neq 0.5\). \(p\) = long-run probability of heads on one flip.

  5. (b) The population proportion assumed under \(H_0\) for the standard error.

  6. The p-value — reject \(H_0\) if p-value \(<\alpha\).

  7. Descriptive: \(\hat{p}\) or counts. Inferential: plausible range for population proportion.

    1. Numerical. b) Categorical, not binary. c) Categorical, binary. d) Numerical.

Section D — Mean and median by hand (Read 2 §3)

D1. Mean and median

Data: \(3, 5, 5, 7, 9\). Compute the mean and median.

Click to reveal answers — Section D — Mean and median by hand (Read 2 §3)
  1. Median \(=5\). Mean \(=(3+5+5+7+9)/5=5.8\).

## Section C — z-scores & statistics toolbox (Read 2 §1–2)

### C1. Annarose z-score

Women: $\mu=64$, $\sigma=2.5$ in. Annarose is 67 in. Compute her z-score.

### C2. Compare z-scores

Newton: 72 in; men $\mu=70$, $\sigma=3$. Who is more unusually tall relative to their group — Annarose ($z=1.2$) or Newton?

### C3. Standard normal probability

For $Z\sim N(0,1)$, what does $P(Z\leq 1.24)$ represent? Also find $P(Z>1.24)$ (table or calculator).

### C4. Coin test hypotheses

16 heads in 20 flips; test if coin is fair (two-sided). State $H_0$, $H_a$, and define $p$ in words.

### C5. Role of $p_0$

In $z=(\hat{p}-p_0)/\sqrt{p_0(1-p_0)/n}$, what is $p_0$?

### C6. prop.test() decision

When using `prop.test()` for a hypothesis test, which output value do you compare to $\alpha$?

### C7. Descriptive vs inferential

One binary variable from a sample: give one **descriptive** summary and one **inferential** question answered by a CI.

### C8. Variable types

Classify each as numerical or categorical (and binary if categorical):

a) Stem diameter (cm)  
b) Soil type: sand/loam/clay  
c) Parasite present: yes/no  
d) Egg count per nest

::: {.content-visible when-format="html"}
<details>
<summary>Click to reveal answers — Section C — z-scores & statistics toolbox (Read 2 §1–2)</summary>

1. $z=(67-64)/2.5=1.2$ — 1.2 SD above the women's mean.

2. Newton: $z=(72-70)/3\approx 0.67$. **Annarose** (larger z-score).

3. Proportion **at or below** 1.24 (about 0.893). $P(Z>1.24)\approx 0.107$.

4. $H_0:p=0.5$, $H_a:p\neq 0.5$. $p$ = long-run probability of heads on one flip.

5. **(b)** The population proportion **assumed under $H_0$** for the standard error.

6. The **p-value** — reject $H_0$ if p-value $<\alpha$.

7. Descriptive: $\hat{p}$ or counts. Inferential: plausible **range for population proportion**.

</details>
